Texas-specific food-system R&D and deployment platform

Lower the delivered cost of nutritious food without spending down Texas' ecological future.

The system treats food price as an end-to-end engineering problem: production + water + aggregation + processing + cold chain + freight + retail + household access. The goal is not autarky. It is stronger regional agency, redundant supply, viable producers and faster cooperation across institutions.

Operating doctrine

  • Measure delivered cost, not ideology. A local product is not automatically cheaper or more resilient.
  • Protect scarce water. Optimize value and nutrition per unit of constrained water, not maximum irrigated acreage.
  • Use existing capacity first. Fill underused kitchens, trucks, coolers and pack lines before building duplicates.
  • Anchor demand before capex. Schools, hospitals, grocers and food banks can make projects financeable when procurement rules allow.
  • Keep trade. Regional sovereignty means agency and redundancy, not closing borders to beneficial trade.

Texas ecology & production geography

Texas spans humid forest, prairie, coast, plateau, semiarid plains and desert. Strategy should follow water, soil, heat, logistics and market context rather than a statewide template.

Why this matters: TPWD describes rainfall from roughly 8 inches in far-west desert to roughly 56 inches in east-Texas swamps. A water-intensive system that is plausible in one region may be irresponsible or uneconomic in another.

Water architecture

For much of Texas, food resilience is a water-allocation problem. The hierarchy starts with measurement and management before expensive new supply.

Planning rule: Compare gross margin, nutrition or marketable output per acre-foot alongside yield per acre. Deep groundwater, reuse and desalination options can be valuable, but their energy, quality, concentrate and ecological costs must be explicit.

Data sovereignty

  • No built-in analytics or telemetry.
  • IndexedDB stores browser-local state; export creates a portable JSON backup.
  • Remote collaboration is peer-to-peer after Trystero discovery; a local BroadcastChannel fallback supports workshops/testing.
  • Room participants can receive shared records. Do not put secrets, personal health data, credentials or regulated records into the shared workspace.
  • There is no automatic cross-device persistence unless a peer or user exports/restores state.

Zero-Harm / Anti-Inversion

Use the platform to increase affordable, safe, voluntary access to food and shared capacity. Do not weaponize scarcity, manipulate vulnerable households, expose private farm/business information, bypass food-safety controls or turn resilience infrastructure into coercive surveillance.

Measures should reward real public benefit: lower avoidable cost, reduced waste, reliable access, producer viability, water stewardship and ecological durability.

Networking limitations

Trystero needs a discovery medium to exchange WebRTC session information. Once peers connect, application data is sent directly peer-to-peer and encrypted by WebRTC. Some network combinations block direct P2P; a TURN relay can be configured. A room code is not identity verification. Use a shared password and an out-of-band trust process for private groups.
